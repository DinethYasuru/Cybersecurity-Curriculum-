Wordlist Builder

See how a "dictionary" of likely passwords is built from a few personal details. The point is to understand why guessable passwords fall, so you can choose ones that don't.

This is a teaching tool

Attackers don't guess passwords one by one. They build a wordlist from things they can find out about you — your name, your town, your birth year, your pet — then add the small tweaks people think are clever (Password → P4ssw0rd!). This page shows exactly how short that list is. That is the lesson: if a tweak is obvious to you, it is already on the list.

Everything runs in your browser. Nothing you type is sent anywhere, saved, or used to attack any real account. Use it only on details you invent for class, or your own.

The takeaway for class. Every option above is something an attacker already assumes you did. A name plus a birthday plus "!" is not a strong password — it is a predictable one, and predictable is exactly what a wordlist catches. What beats all of this is length and randomness: a long passphrase of unrelated words, or a password manager that makes a different random one for every account.
